Executive Summary & Overview

Department of EXTC • Mini Project Exhibition and Competition
14 May 2026
Bridging Theoretical Foundations with Hands-On Engineering

SANKALPANA 2026 was a Mini Project Exhibition and Competition successfully organized by the Department of Electronics and Telecommunication Engineering (EXTC) at Don Bosco Institute of Technology (DBIT), Kurla, Mumbai. Held on 14 May 2026, the event brought together 119 Second Year (SE) and Third Year (TE) students who designed, built, and demonstrated functional mini-projects across embedded systems, IoT, automation, digital electronics, and communication systems.

The initiative was organized under the banner of IETE-DBIT-ISF and coordinated by Mr. Ankur Ganorkar. The event served as a vibrant experiential platform bridging the gap between rigorous theoretical classroom learning and practical field engineering, while equipping future engineers with essential communication, problem-solving, and collaborative skills.

Event Objectives
  • Provide a dedicated platform for SE and TE students to showcase innovative mini projects and technical concepts.
  • Encourage students to translate theoretical principles into practical engineering applications using microcontrollers, sensors, and communication modules.
  • Promote creativity, collaborative teamwork, and analytical problem-solving through project-based learning.
  • Give students rigorous exposure to technical viva, project defense, and formal evaluation by expert faculty judges.
  • Motivate exploration of modern toolchains such as ESP32, Arduino, FPGA, digital ICs, and sensor telemetry.
Key Outcomes
  • Participants successfully demonstrated working hardware prototypes across embedded systems, IoT, automation, and digital logic.
  • Students solidified their practical understanding of PCB/circuit design, sensor interfacing, and real-time firmware development.
  • Participants developed sharp technical presentation skills and confidence when articulating engineering decisions to evaluators.
  • Enhanced inter-team collaboration, innovation spirit, and readiness for advanced final-year capstone projects.

Key Strengths Observed
  • Academic: High-quality student innovation at SE and TE levels demonstrating strong technical foundations.
  • Integration: Effective hands-on synthesis of embedded microcontrollers, sensors, and FPGA description logic.
  • Diversity: Interdisciplinary project spectrum encompassing IoT, automation, medical assistive devices, and signal telemetry.
  • Operational: Seamless coordination across registration, labs, jury evaluation, and the valedictory ceremony.
Roadmap & Recommendations
  • Short-Term: Conduct structured pre-event mentoring bootcamps and standardize judging rubrics across cohorts.
  • Medium-Term: Invite industry evaluators and expand an inter-collegiate dimension with incubator support for high-potential prototypes.
  • Long-Term: Institutionalize Sankalpana into the department's Outcome-Based Education (OBE) strategy with seed funding grants.
